Flagship · System

Copy governance systemA closed loop from Figma to a living canon — two tools with a self-updating source of truth between them.

The problem

The product ships copy across three platforms from one Figma source that doubles as dev handoff. At ~1,600 strings, four things kept breaking:

  • Drift. The same string worded differently across platforms; regressions reintroduced in new versions.
  • Canon rot. The voice & tone guide lived in Notion as a doc nobody re-read; review decisions never made it back in.
  • Bottleneck. Every “is this on-brand?” routed through one writer. Copy QA didn’t scale past one person.
  • No line on pressure. Where persuasion ends and pressure begins got decided screen by screen, by whoever wrote it — nobody was tracking it as a class.

What I built

Deterministic · scale

copy-parityNode.js · Figma REST API

extract→ pair→ lint→ report→ comment
  • Pulls every text node, pairs equivalents across platforms, lints each against rules.yaml — the copy canon as code, where adding a rule means one regex in a config file.
  • Posts findings back onto Figma frames as node-anchored comments. Read + comment only — never mutates the canvas.
  • Catches the machine-checkable class across 1,600+ strings in one pass: casing, broken tokens, drift, banned constructions.
Judgment · on-demand

ux-copy-auditAI skill · Notion via MCP

screenshot in→ check canon→ before/after out
  • Anyone — PM, designer, writer — drops a screenshot or Figma frame. No briefing; the skill carries the context.
  • Reads the live authority stack from Notion: the UX Copy Patterns DB (Status = Active), tone-of-voice pillars, naming rules — then judges the screen against canon.
  • Returns a before/after with rationale. Approved calls graduate back into the Patterns DB and land in Field Notes with per-writer attribution.

Case study · Growth

Getting returning users back inA web re-entry flow nobody owned. I found the drop-off, scoped the fix with engineering, got it onto the roadmap and shipped it as an A/B test.

My role

I spotted the problem and drove it to release: sized it with analytics, chose the solution with engineering, pitched it to the PO, and built the new flow with a designer.

The problem

People coming back to the web product had to get through 15 screens to reach an account they already had. The flow was long, its order didn’t match what people came back to do, and the copy on each screen was vague. The designer and I flagged it, and analytics confirmed the drop-off. Everyone lost here had already chosen the product and simply couldn’t get back in.

Chosen

Magic linkone tap from the inbox

  • Fewest steps for the user: no password, no code to copy between apps.
  • Engineering rated it the easier of the two to build, so it fit into one planning cycle.
Considered

One-time codecode sent by email

  • Also removes the password, but the user still switches apps and types a code.
  • More work to implement, which would have pushed the test back.
Hypothesis

If we simplify the welcome-back flow and cut the actions needed to log in, more users will successfully return to the product.

Results

The test won on every metric we tracked, and the effect carried all the way to the paid step. The new flow went out to all users.

Relative lift vs. control, rounded. All three results statistically significant.

What I’d check next

Some of the paid-reading lift came from returning users spending credits they had bought earlier, so not all of it is new revenue. The next numbers I’d track are new purchases among returning users and their D7 retention.

Most of the work happened before any copy was written: finding the drop-off, sizing it, and getting engineering and the PO behind one option.

Case study
Case study · Trust

Trust by designResonance without pressure — in a product built on hope, for people who arrive at vulnerable moments.

People come to a psychic-chat product mid-breakup, in grief, or facing a hard decision. That’s the moment copy has to earn trust rather than spend it — and the frictionless version always pulls harder than it should. I held the line between resonance and pressure on every surface a user meets — the conversation, the promos, and the money flow — and encoded it into the system so the line holds without me.

Part 1 · In the conversation
Auditing a chatbot intake funnel where the whole conversation is the unit of analysis

The brief was “here’s the flow, fix the copy” — a ~220-string chatbot funnel steering new users toward a trial psychic chat. I reframed the problem before touching a word.

A line can be perfect alone and broken in sequence — “Got it.” landing four times in a row. So the unit of audit isn’t the string. It’s the assembled dialogue.
Path-level finding · assembled transcript
answers gender→Got it.
answers status→Got it.
taps “Other”→Got it.
answers focus→Got it — this is an internal journey.
Each line is fine on its own. Four “Got it” openers in a row read like a machine — and nothing in a string-by-string review would ever surface it. The fix below changes what each reaction is doing, step by step.

I ran two passes: a string-level pass, and a path-level pass — programmatically assembling seven end-to-end transcripts and reading them as real conversations for rhythm, repetition, and cumulative tone. The audit framework had no conversational lenses, so I built five and added them as a reusable pattern family:

PersonaTurn designSequence rhythmPromise vs. intentBot → human handoff
−One of the most common and most painful fears. You're not alone in it.
+So many people secretly carry "am I enough" — you're not alone in it.
Vulnerable-moment reaction. “Common” quietly devalues the very fear the user just admitted. Normalize without diminishing.
−Your psychic will show you where closure actually comes from.
+Waiting for closure that never came — that ache is real. It deserves a real answer.
Outcome guarantee → process. Process verbs (read, tune into, look at) are fine; guaranteeing an outcome is a hard voice prohibition for an entertainment-only service.
−The future feels uncertain right now. I want to understand why.
POV bug (flagged 🔴). The bot speaks in the user’s own voice, then answers itself. Persona integrity: the assistant prepares the question, the human psychic reads.

The lead moment — give each line a job

After the PM signed off, the real question surfaced: how are these responses even formed? The whole corpus ran on one generator — mirror the user, weigh the feeling, bridge to the psychic — so it read like a catalog of captions. The assistant never responded — it evaluated. The fix wasn’t another polish pass. It was giving authors a palette of conversational jobs and a quota per path.

User taps: “I work hard but I’m invisible and overlooked”
Mirror

Being good at what you do and still not being seen — that chips away at you.

Presence

That’s an exhausting way to work. I’m glad you said it straight.

Normalize

More people carry this one than ever say it out loud.

Silence · bridge

Noted — word for word. This goes to your psychic as is.

One option, four different jobs. The rule: on a ~7-reaction path — 3 mirrors · 2 presence · 1 normalize · 1 bridge. A constitution for the copy — the rules authors write to. And it’s a trust question, not only a style one: a corpus that weighs every feeling on the same scale is performing care rather than offering it, and users hear the difference.

Part 3 · In the money flow
Holding the line in the money flow

Money flows are where the canon has to be strictest — the stakes are highest, and every subscription product feels the pull to press harder there. Four patterns came out of the standards work; three are now encoded as lint rules, so every future instance gets flagged without me re-reading a single screen.

1Confirmshaming caught by rules.yaml
Before

Before you go

Your subscription renews on June 14. Cancel now and you keep access until then.

We can also add one free week to your plan — no charge, no commitment.

Add a free week
I don’t want free week of usage — I want to cancel my subscription
After

Before you go

Your subscription renews on June 14. Cancel now and you keep access until then.

We can also add one free week to your plan — no charge, no commitment.

Add a free week
Cancel subscription
Guilt as the price of leaving. An exit link worded this way makes the user decline a gift out loud to cancel. The offer stays — it just stops being the toll on the exit.
2Manufactured urgency caught by rules.yaml
Before

Don’t miss your chat: share your phone number to get instant notifications

We’ll text you when your psychic replies, so you don’t have to keep the app open.

+1 (555) 000-0000
Continue
Not now
After

Get notified when your psychic replies

We’ll text you when your psychic replies, so you don’t have to keep the app open.

+1 (555) 000-0000
Continue
Not now
Urgency doing the work a benefit should do. Nothing is expiring, and what’s being asked for is personal data. Lead with the benefit, then ask. The same rule catches “Don’t miss out on a single perk” and “Don’t miss a thing.”
3Manufactured anxiety → upsell
−Your energy is blocked but we have the tools to help you. Explore … our Qi Gong video course and shield yourself from energy drains.
+Feeling drained is common — some people find breathwork helps. Explore our Qi Gong course.
A diagnosis a product can’t make, used as the reason to buy. Naming a feeling is fine; asserting a blocked energy state and charging for the cure isn’t. No regex catches this one — it takes a read.
4Directive pressure caught by rules.yaml
−You have to accept
+Accept to continue
“Have to / must / should” removes the user’s standing to decline. The directive-modality rule flags all three across the product.
Encoded once, enforced everywhere

Three of the four aren’t found by reading screens one at a time. confirmshaming, urgency-scarcity and directive-modality are rules I encoded in rules.yaml, so the linter flags every instance on every screen — including the ones written after me.
